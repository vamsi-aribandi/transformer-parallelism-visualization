A Visual Guide to Parallel Transformers

How a transformer's forward and backward passes are sharded across devices — data, tensor, context, pipeline, and expert parallelism, step by step, in the notation of the JAX scaling book.

How to read the figures

Each figure is a small machine you can drive. Model depth runs left to right: the input enters at In, passes through each layer's attention and MLP stations, and leaves at Out. Devices are horizontal lanes, stacked vertically — when devices communicate, tensors fly vertically between lanes. Weights are blue, activations amber, keys and values teal, and gradients rose. A dashed outline is the ghost of a tensor's full logical extent: the solid patch inside it is the slice this device holds, drawn at that device's actual offset. Hover any tensor for its shape, sharding, and memory at our nominal sizes (B=8, T=128, D=1024, F=4096, bf16).

The step bar under each figure is discrete — every segment is one semantic operation (a matmul, a collective, a save). Click a segment to jump to the state after that operation; press play to watch the operations animate in order. The equation under the canvas is the current operation in scaling-book notation; during the backward pass a second line cites the forward equation it is the derivative of. Each strategy opens with its forward pass; the backward pass — twice the compute, and the reason activations are saved — unfolds when you're ready.

Tensor parallelism

Tensor parallelism (the Megatron scheme) shards the feature dimensions: attention heads across devices (W_qkv[D, H_Y], W_o[H_Y, D]) and the MLP's hidden width (W_in[D, F_Y], W_out[F_Y, D]). Activations travel sharded on D. Every block then follows one rhythm: AllGather on the way in, ReduceScatter on the way out. The first matmul needs the full D, so the sharded activations are gathered; the second matmul contracts a sharded dimension, so each device is left holding an unreduced partial sum — the dashed {U_Y} tensor — which the ReduceScatter resolves while re-sharding for the next block.

Watch the backward pass mirror the forward exactly: the gradient arrives where the output left, the forward's ReduceScatter becomes an AllGather of dOut, the forward's AllGather becomes a ReduceScatter of dX, and each weight's gradient (dW = Xᵀ·dY) pulses the activation that was saved beneath it. Weight gradients stay local — in pure TP nothing sums across the batch, because every device saw the whole batch.
